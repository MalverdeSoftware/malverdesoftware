# Jesús Malverde / Jesús Maaso: literature

Why this is here: MalverdeSoftware is named to honor Jesús Malverde, and [the brand ethos](../brand/DESIGN-ETHOS.md) names him as Jesús Maaso, Yoreme/Yoeme. This folder holds what published sources say about him, so that wording can rest on known sources.

## What the sources say

### The name

| Form | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| Jesús Juárez Mazo | Most sources, as the name given by oral tradition (Price 2005; Flores and González 2006; Perea Romo 2020; Sotelo Chávez 2021) |
| Jesús Juárez Mazzo | Da Cunha Rocha 2019; Rodríguez 2003 |
| Jesús Mazo | Fabián Jiménez 2025 |
| Jesús Malverde, son of Guadalupe Malverde | The 1888 Paredones civil record |
| Jesús Juárez Matzo Campos; Jesús Malverde Campos | English and Spanish Wikipedia |

**No source on Malverde uses "Maaso".** The closest forms in the Malverde literature are Mazo and Mazzo. In the oral account recorded by Perea Romo, Mazo is the surname of his mother, Chavelita Mazo.

**Maaso is a documented Yoeme family name.** Your Zotero holds Maaso, Molina and Evers (1993), which records Miki Maaso, a Yoeme deer singer from the Rio Yaqui area, and Luis Maaso. Molina and Evers (1998) discuss a talk by Miki Maaso. This corrects the first pass, which missed the surname because it searched only Malverde sources and one surname list.

*Maaso* is also the word for deer, in the Universidad Autónoma de Sinaloa's [Mayo-Yoreme corpus](https://mayo-yoreme.uas.edu.mx/public/pages/load/animales_silvestres) and in Yoeme sources such as *Maso Bwikam* (Evers and Molina 1987).

So a Yoeme reading of Mazo as Maaso is plausible, but no source found makes that link for Malverde. It remains an open question, and it would take a family, community or archival source to settle it.

### Yoreme or Yoeme identity

No scholarly work checked says he was Yoreme or Yoeme. The five read for this question (Price 2005, Perea Romo 2020, Da Cunha Rocha 2019, Sotelo Chávez 2021, Fabián Jiménez 2025) attribute no Indigenous identity to him. None of them set out to test the question either, so this silence is not evidence against it.

The connections that did turn up:

1. **Felipe Bachomo.** Calvo-Quirós (2022, p. 35) reports the view that Malverde is an amalgam of two men: Heraclio Bernal, and Felipe Bachomo, an Indigenous rebel of northern Sinaloa. Bachomo was the Yoreme leader of the Río Fuerte uprising, shot at Los Mochis in 1916. This is the strongest scholarly thread to a Yoreme connection, though it makes Malverde partly a composite rather than a Yoreme man. Calvo-Quirós takes the phrase "amalgam of two bandits" from Quinones (2001, p. 227; endnote 10). Quinones was not read, so what it rests on is unknown.
   *Author's note, November 5, 2026:* the author has also heard this link anecdotally. That is oral testimony, kept separate from the published record. It may or may not be independent of Quinones, so who said it and where is worth recording.
2. **A Yoreme rescue.** Calvo-Quirós (pp. 53-54) retells a version in which Mayo/Yoreme people rescue and hide Malverde after he is flogged and left to die, and teach him to survive in the sierra. The source is a play, Óscar Liera's *El jinete de la divina providencia* (1984; endnote 39), so this is a dramatist's telling and not evidence of who Malverde was. Calvo-Quirós reads these figures critically, as outsiders who enter only to help him.
3. **English Wikipedia.** It states that he was of Yoreme and Spanish heritage. That sentence has no citation of its own. The next sentence cites Price 2004, pp. 153-157, which were not checked.
4. **Bachomo's memorial.** Soto Sánchez (2020), quoting Gill, describes a stone mound at Bachomo's execution site, added to over time, and a veneration of him as a Mayo Christ. That closely resembles the stone pile at Malverde's grave. The comparison is ours; no source read draws it beyond the amalgam claim. Verify it in Gill before relying on it.
5. **Social media.** One unattributed Facebook comment says he was of Indigenous origin. It is a lead only and is not in the bibliography.

Nothing found addresses Yoeme/Yaqui identity specifically.

### Whether he existed

Sources disagree, and the question is still open.

- **No evidence:** Price (2005, p. 191) reports no archival evidence for a flesh-and-blood Malverde or Juárez Mazo. Flores and González (2006, p. 32) treat him as more mythic than historical. Da Cunha Rocha (2019, p. 4) says his existence is hard to prove.
- **The 1888 record:** a child named Jesús, natural son of Guadalupe Malverde, registered at Paredones, Culiacán, on January 15, 1888 (AHGES, Libro 66, Actas de Nacimiento, Acta 108, as cited by Perea Romo 2020).
  - Perea Romo finds the document materially genuine. It doesn't match the oral name or the 1870 birth, and the child may have been named after the bandit.
  - Sotelo Chávez (2021, p. 91) says only a register entry was found, and that the record it points to has not been located.
  - Da Cunha Rocha treats it as not guaranteed authentic.
  - The sources also disagree on when it was found: 2004 or 2007.
- **A notarial record:** in a 2021 talk reported by López (*El Debate*), historian Gilberto López Alanís described a document in the Archivo Notarial detailing how Malverde was killed and the reward collected. No archive reference is given. If it exists, this would be the most important source of all.
- **Oral tradition:** born at Las Juntas, Mocorito, on December 24, 1870, to Doroteo Juárez and Chavelita Mazo, and died May 3, 1909 (Lizárraga 1998, via Fabián Jiménez 2025; chapel keeper's interview in Perea Romo 2020). Sotelo Chávez calls the source of the 1909 date unknown.

## What this means for our wording

The ethos currently states as fact that he was Yoreme/Yoeme and names him Jesús Maaso. The published sources found so far don't document either. Honoring him, and saying that this is the author's understanding or that it comes from family or community knowledge, is unaffected. A public statement of fact, or one citing the literature, would need a source that hasn't been found yet. That wording is the author's decision, and nothing in `brand/` was changed.

## What would settle more of this

In rough order of value:

1. Quinones 2001, p. 227, for what the Bachomo claim rests on. Requested from the library in [MVS-14](https://malverdesoftware.youtrack.cloud/issue/MVS-14), along with Griffith 2003, which Calvo-Quirós cites at pp. 66-68 (endnote 11).
2. Price 2004, pp. 153-157, to test the Wikipedia sentence. Also in MVS-14.
3. Archivo Histórico General del Estado de Sinaloa: Libro 66, the Jesús Malverde file named by Perea Romo, and the Archivo Notarial document López Alanís described.
4. Jónsdóttir 2014, Valenzuela Arce and Valenzuela Arámburo 2023, García 2017 (published in Mocorito), and Ortiz Castillo 2012.
5. Yoreme and Yoeme knowledge holders, and the descendants in Lomas Blancas that Rodríguez (2003) mentions. Oral and community knowledge is largely missing from what these indexes cover, and it may be where this question gets answered.
